docs/data-sources.md

The list below is the original research index. It includes optional references,
not a requirement to download every source. See the Markdown registry for the
current decisions and the corrected Electricity Maps documentation URL.

1. EV charging sessions and demand
https://ev.caltech.edu/dataset.html from Caltech, REAL charging-session records including connection/disconnection times, delivered energy, charger identifiers, and selected time-series measurements. JSON download, REST API, Python client; API registration required.
Useful for: realistic vehicle arrivals, connection durations, charging requirements, and scheduling workloads.

https://developer.nlr.gov/docs/transportation/evi-pro-lite-v1/ from National Laboratory of the rockies, MODEL-generated EV fleet charging load profiles for representative weekdays and weekends, including 15-minute power-demand intervals. API key for higher limits.
Useful for: Estimate aggregate charging demand and create different depot scenarios.

2. Electricity demand and grid conditions
https://www.data.open-power-system-data.org/time_series/ from Jonathan Mühlenpfordt @Neon.  European power demand, wind and solar time series
Useful for: Background grid conditions and variable supply.

https://www.eia.gov/opendata/ from Energy Information Administration, Actual and forecast electricity demand, generation and interchange.
Useful for: Regional demand and grid-stress scenarios

3. Electricity prices and tariffs
https://transparency.entsoe.eu/ from European Network of Transmission System Operators for Electricity, European wholesale electricity market prices. Registration/API access.

https://www.data.open-power-system-data.org/time_series/ from same institute as above, Historical price series for selected markets and periods. Downloadable CSV.

4. Solar generation and renewable energy
https://developer.nlr.gov/docs/solar/nsrdb/ from NLR, Solar irradiance and weather time series.
Useful for: Simulation of rooftop solar production.

https://power.larc.nasa.gov/ from NASA, Historical solar and meteorological data.
Useful for Alternative location-based renewable profiles.
