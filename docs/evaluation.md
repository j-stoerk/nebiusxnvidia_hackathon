# Evidence contract (defined before real-data experiments)

Target: German fleet/depot operators choosing capacity **before committing capex**. The proposed differentiator is an auditable capacity frontier, uncertainty evaluation, and solver-checked alternatives. It is a hypothesis, not an established market gap.

## Locked hypotheses

Use one site, six consecutive months where possible, with train/calibration/test calendar blocks (first 50% / next 25% / final 25%). No random session split. Freeze code, inputs, tariff, solver budget, seed 20261024, and this protocol's SHA-256 before the first test run. Do not tune on test results.

The first available snapshot is Office 1 (1,481 sessions, 2019-03-25–2021-04-12), obtained from the [research authors' public ACN mirror](https://github.com/yvenn-amara/ev-load-open-data). This spans pandemic disruption and must not be described as an exchangeable depot sample. Direct Caltech requests timed out. Source JSON and normalized CSV checksums identify the mirror; personal fields are stripped and raw exports are not retained. The initial exploratory replay will use **assumed 6.6 kW per port, eight recorded ports, a 52.8 kW site ceiling, efficiency 1, unknown SoC**, and a **2026 German low-voltage network-charge sensitivity** (2.73 ct/kWh + €15.20/kW/month). These are explicit scenario assumptions, not measured site equipment, historical prices, or a complete electricity bill. Do not publish this as a confirmatory pilot result. Real tariff/connection and vehicle data must replace the assumptions for a deployment claim.

1. H1: the optimizer does not increase paired daily energy-not-served against FCFS, EDF, or LLF (tolerance 1e-5 kWh).
2. H2: with a demand tariff, paired peak kW and incremental demand cost decrease against each comparator. Failure to improve counts as a null result.
3. H3: total energy plus incremental demand cost decreases **at equal delivered energy**. Always report energy-not-served alongside costs; do not call undercharging savings.
4. H4: a smaller connection/charger candidate serves every departure on the held-out duty cycles, using fixed plug occupancy. Report an observed feasible candidate, not future reliability or proven avoided capex. Cost avoidance needs actual connection/equipment quotes and existing site load; lead-time avoidance needs a DSO quote.
5. H5: one-sided departure-lower / energy-upper conformal bounds attain at least 95% joint empirical coverage on the final chronological block; report their individual and joint coverage, interval widths, and pinball losses. Separately measure actual readiness from executed charging. Coverage is not readiness.

Report absolute metrics and paired optimizer-minus-comparator differences with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. Resample contiguous seven-day blocks, keeping every algorithm's observations paired; use 2,000 replicates and state the number of test days/blocks. These are per-comparison intervals without family-wise multiplicity correction, not simultaneous proof of all hypotheses. Short demo runs are functional checks, not scientific evidence. Include solver timeouts, infeasible cases, missing sessions and excluded records; never discard poor outcomes. Label retrospective schedules as a daily rolling oracle: actual labels and arrivals through day-end, with overnight energy preserved and separate billing-peak ledgers per policy. They must not be mixed with causal MPC results.

Replay pre-test sessions as a warm-up to establish physical occupancy, delivered energy and billing peaks; only complete UTC days after the test cutoff enter the paired intervals. Cross-boundary labels are excluded from training/calibration, not silently treated as known-at-arrival inputs. The batch evaluator permits seven-day working horizons for long/overnight stays; the interactive upload limit remains 72 hours. If longer stays or invalid physical records prevent replay, record an incomplete evaluation instead of silently removing them.

## Physics and information

FCFS, EDF and LLF share station/connector limits, time grid, efficiency, taper and occupancy assumptions. EDF orders departures; LLF recomputes time-to-deadline minus minimum remaining charging time each interval. Tie-breaks are deterministic. Fixed occupancy is the normal operator mode, and explicit plug bindings survive outages and reserve idle connected vehicles. Our implementation follows those policies; it is not ACN-Sim itself and omits its unbalanced phase network, shared DC cabinets and pilot quantization. These need real equipment/network specifications; nameplate limits alone do not establish site commissioning compliance.

Optional capacity/initial SoC enable a two-stage CC/CV acceptance curve: constant ceiling below transition SoC, then a linearly declining ceiling. Constant interval dispatch is constrained at terminal SoC, a conservative approximation valid throughout the interval. It does not reproduce an unconstrained exponential current trace exactly. Without measured SoC/capacity, keep constant-power mode and report that limitation; never infer chemistry from a session's kWh alone.

ACN contains real workplace sessions, **not fleet duty cycles**. Disconnect time and delivered kWh are observed labels/proxies, not departure/energy requirements known at arrival. Missing requested-energy fields are not fabricated. Do not use future `userInputs`, disconnects, observed charging power, or completed kWh as online features. Battery-side versus meter-side energy must use an explicit efficiency assumption. A depot dataset needs vehicle identity, availability known at decision time, route/roster deadlines, energy requirements, meter/site load, equipment/SoC and source timestamps. Personal identifiers and raw sessions stay outside Git.

## Tariff checks

[Energienetze Berlin's 2026 price sheet](https://www.energienetze-berlin.de/download-service.html?file=files%2Fenergienetze-berlin.de%2Fpdf%2FEnergienetze+Berlin+GmbH%2FNetzzugang+%2B+Entgelte%2FNetznutzungsentgelte_2026.pdf) defines 15-minute mean demand and annual/monthly systems. Its low-voltage monthly capacity component is €15.20/kW/month net, plus 2.73 ct/kWh network energy; supply, taxes and other charges are additional. Annual systems depend on utilization. This verifies the mechanism, not that the tariff applies to a particular depot or that demand savings dominate there. A 10 kW reduction in an otherwise new monthly billed peak would reduce this component by €152; it saves nothing if an earlier peak still determines the bill.

The implementation accounts for **incremental** peak charges over supplied prior peaks, grouped by Europe/Berlin month/year (including DST; UTC is optional), and splits partial dispatch at metering windows. It excludes building load, tariff switching, ratchets, time-window tariffs, subscriptions and taxes. Check the actual meter's billing calendar before making billing claims. A short horizon is a partial-period calculation, never an annual bill. Default demand price is zero until explicitly configured. Do not silently combine California sessions and German tariffs into a claimed observed German deployment.

## Risk and control gates

The conformal module is an offline baseline: training-only point predictions and calibration-only residual quantiles, with alpha/2 allocated to each one-sided bound. Separate absolute-residual intervals report two-sided joint coverage and finite interval widths. The nominal 95% **marginal joint** statement depends on exchangeability; correlated time series and distribution shifts can invalidate it. See [Xu & Xie, SPCI](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/xu23r/xu23r.pdf). Reporting empirical coverage is mandatory. A chance-constrained controller additionally needs a feasible robust plan, a justified risk allocation (per vehicle versus entire fleet), observed departures/SoC, calibrated online updates and executed first-step evaluation. Do not display `P(ready by departure) >= 95%` in the demo without that evidence.

The existing simulation replans on time advances/events. It is deterministic receding-horizon planning, not yet calibrated chance-constrained MPC. Typed roster proposals are read-only previews with source quotes. Solvers validate feasibility; an independent validator checks schedules; neither certifies an LLM's interpretation. Counterfactuals use explicit re-solves, not integer-program duals or an alleged minimal infeasible subsystem.

Next control sequence: simulator-tested OCPP adapter (explicit protocol version, station/connector bindings, acknowledgements/rejections/timeouts, stale-profile clearing, meter feedback); then PV/battery optimization with site energy balance, inverter/SoC limits, efficiencies and degradation; then V2G with eligible vehicles, authorization, export limits/tariffs and departure reserve. These are not implemented or hardware-certified by the planning demo.
